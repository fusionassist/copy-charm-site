Most people start the digital signage conversation with the wrong question: "what size screen do
I need?". The right starting point is "what do I want a person standing 4 metres away to do?"

## Three questions before any spec

1. **Who's looking?** A queuing customer with 90 seconds is not a passing pedestrian with 4. The
   message, motion, and screen position change completely.
2. **What's the room?** Direct sunlight needs 2,500+ nits. A dim café needs 350. Glare from a
   shop window changes the answer again.
3. **Who's updating the content?** A screen with bad content is worse than no screen. If nobody
   has 30 minutes a week to refresh it, you don't have a signage project — you have a poster.

## Brightness, in normal English

| Setting | Nits | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Café interior | 350 | The default for desk monitors. Fine indoors away from windows. |
| Shop interior with windows | 700 | Won't wash out under ambient daylight. |
| Shop window facing out | 2,500 | High-bright. Stays readable behind glass in direct sun. |
| Outdoor shopfront / forecourt | 3,500+ | True outdoor-rated, weatherproof enclosure required. |

If you're getting 350-nit screens for a shop window, you've been mis-sold. We see it weekly.

## The mount matters more than people think

A screen on a flimsy bracket telegraphs "temporary". A flush-mount on the wall reads as part of
the space. A freestanding totem is moveable but takes floor footprint. Get the right one and the
hardware disappears — get the wrong one and the screen reads as an afterthought.
